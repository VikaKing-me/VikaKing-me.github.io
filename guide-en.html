Amelia: everything you need to get started

Your guide from Bean & Pop, created by Mama Needs Caffeine & Co.

In this guide you'll find: what Amelia is, why you get paid to do tasks, the gear you need, how to record, examples of home and business tasks, payments, referrals, tips, and answers to frequently asked questions.

Learn how Amelia works and how to start without paying a sign-up fee!

Before you start

  • Always follow the current rules in the official Amelia app. Requirements, rates, tasks, and availability can change.
  • Recordings must be made with a compatible phone and an approved head strap, with the camera pointing down.
  • This guide is an independent resource; it is not an official communication from Amelia.

What is Amelia?

Amelia is an artificial-intelligence training platform. It needs recordings of everyday tasks so that AI and robotics systems can learn how real-world activities, errands, and jobs are done.

Residential tasks

  • Wash dishes; prepare food; fold laundry; clean and organize.
  • Pack items; work in the garden; care for pets.
  • Run errands or go shopping and other approved activities.

Commercial tasks

  • Planting and agriculture; device repairs.
  • Cleaning hotels and other workspaces.
  • Gardening and green-space maintenance.
  • Food preparation in commercial kitchens, where allowed.

How to start: 4 steps

  • Sign up: use the sign-up link and create your account.
  • Complete onboarding: review Amelia's email and instructions.
  • Record tasks: use the phone in the head strap while doing approved activities.
  • Get help: use the support and resources available in the app.

What exactly are we doing?

1. What are these tasks for?

We help train AI and robotics systems to perform real activities. A first-person view of the hands completing everyday tasks is recorded. The data is used to train models that can understand and perform those activities.

2. Do I have to set aside extra time?

One advantage is that you can record tasks that were already part of your routine, as long as they follow Amelia's rules.

  • Preparing meals, cleaning up after the kids, setting the table, or making the bed.
  • Buying groceries, putting the groceries away, cleaning and organizing the house.
  • Packing bags for a trip or a move.

3. How does it work?

  • Choose an eligible task in the app.
  • Place the phone in the head strap with the camera pointing down.
  • Complete the activity normally and follow the prompts.
  • Submit the recorded time for review and approval.

4. Why can it be a practical side gig?

Instead of adding another activity to a full schedule, you can try to earn compensation for household tasks you were already going to do. Payments depend on approved hours and current policies.

What do I need to get started?

1. The basics

  • A compatible smartphone.
  • A head strap or mount that holds the phone centered on your forehead.
  • Choosing a compatible, safe model.

2. Two strap options

  • Option 1 (recommended): amzn.to/4gUaehW
  • Option 2 (most comfortable by far): amzn.to/4gUgmqo

3. Phones listed in the guide

  • iPhone 11 to iPhone 17 (check exceptions and updates in the app).
  • Google Pixel 6 to Pixel 9.
  • Samsung Galaxy S21 to S26 (check exceptions and variants).

Not allowed: holding the phone in your hand to record; using Meta glasses or smart glasses; using a chest mount. The camera must point down from the forehead.

4. When can I record?

Tasks may be available at different times of day. If a task shows as available and you meet its rules, you can do it.

5. How to avoid a rejected recording

  • Keep the camera pointed down and your hands visible most of the time.
  • Make sure the camera and mount are positioned well and the lens is clean.
  • Avoid recording other people and follow Amelia's privacy rules.

Gear and reimbursement

The phone records what you do from your point of view. A strap placed on your forehead keeps your hands free and helps produce a more stable recording.

Allowed and not-allowed straps

  • Yes: a compatible head strap that holds the phone securely.
  • No: Meta glasses, smart glasses, or chest straps.

Where do I make the recordings?

Tasks are recorded inside the Minute app; they are not posted to your social media.

1. Download the app

Download Minute as directed by Amelia.

2. Press “Record”

Open the app and start recording following its instructions.

3. Do your task

Place the phone on your forehead, pointing down, and complete an approved task.

Recording length

  • Minimum length indicated: 1 minute.
  • Maximum length per clip indicated: 30 minutes.
  • You can record more than one session to complete a task, following the app's rules.

Camera and privacy

  • The phone must go in a head strap, with the camera pointing down.
  • Children must not appear in the video. If they come near, make sure they aren't recorded.
  • Do not use chest mounts or Meta glasses.

Is background noise allowed?

Normal household noise is fine! The systems can learn to operate in everyday environments with real sounds.

Examples of normal sounds

  • TV or music.
  • Children playing, as long as they don't appear in the recording.
  • Dogs barking or other animal noises.
  • Conversations, within applicable privacy limits.
  • Vacuum, dishwasher, washer, or dryer.
  • Fans, air conditioning, and general household noise.

Do your everyday activities in a normal setting. Check the app for special situations and avoid recording other people's private information.

Payments: frequently asked questions

1. How much do they pay me?

Updated monthly rates:

0–99 approved hours in the month: $10 per hour.

100 approved hours or more in the month: $11 per hour.

The rate is based on approved hours and the current monthly policy. Check the app for the most current information.

2. When do the levels reset?

At the start of each month the monthly pay level resets. Hours accumulated for certain processes may carry over according to the official rules, though the hourly level starts over.

3. How do the increments work?

In the guide's information, earnings are handled in 5-hour increments. Check the rules and approved hours in your account.

4. When do I request payment?

The guide indicates that payments can be requested on Mondays and are normally sent on Fridays, subject to review and meeting the requirements. A minimum of $100 in task earnings is indicated to request payment.

5. Are there other ways to earn?

Amelia may offer challenges, bonuses, or incentives subject to change. Always confirm the current conditions before counting on that income.

How does the pay structure work?

  • Level 1: 0–99 hours per month = $10/hour.
  • Level 2: 100 hours or more per month = $11/hour.

Important: pay levels, bonus conditions, referral terms, and app rules can change. The official app prevails.

Residential task list

Patio and garden

  • Arrange patio furniture; clean gutters; rake or blow leaves.
  • Plant or pull weeds; clean the pool; spread compost or fertilizer.
  • Stack firewood; sweep the porch; prune shrubs; water plants; pressure-wash exterior surfaces.

Home projects and maintenance

  • Assemble furniture; hang pictures or mirrors; put up curtains.
  • Put up seasonal decorations; change light bulbs or batteries.
  • Replace a showerhead; adjust hinges or cabinets; water indoor plants.

Bedroom, kitchen, and bathroom

  • Tidy the bedroom, change sheets, and make the bed.
  • Clean the kitchen; take out the trash; put away groceries; make coffee or tea; set the table.
  • Load or unload the dishwasher; prepare or cook food.
  • Clean the toilet and tidy the bathroom.

Laundry, vehicle, and organization

  • Fold, hang, air-dry, hand-wash, or put clothes in the washer.
  • Check or add engine oil; change a tire; check or add air.
  • Clean the car; organize the garage; pack or unpack the car for a trip.
  • Tidy the desk; put gas in the car; go shopping.

Pets and interactive tasks

  • Clean the litter box, feed a pet, and walk the dog.
  • Interactive tasks can change; check the current list in Minute.

Commercial task list

These are categories and examples from the original guide. Only record at a business with the necessary authorization and if the task is approved in the app.

Agriculture and nurseries

Plant, harvest, and sort crops; greenhouse and nursery work; landscaping and grounds maintenance.

Hotels and hospitality

Clean rooms and bathrooms; change bed linens; laundry; restock supplies; clean common areas; pool maintenance and luggage handling.

Restaurants and kitchens

Cook, prepare food, clean the kitchen area, take out trash or recycling, wash dishes, disinfect surfaces, and restock supplies.

Retail stores

Restock shelves; arrange displays; prepare orders; pack and label; restock supplies and clean common areas and restrooms.

Offices and commercial cleaning

Clean offices, vacuum carpets, dust, clean windows and glass, empty trash, restock supplies, and disinfect high-touch areas.

Automotive, construction, and warehouses

Dry and polish cars; clean interiors; vacuum vehicles; wash cars, change oil, or inflate tires. Tidy job sites, install materials, paint, repair, load and unload trucks, prepare orders, pack and organize inventory.

Events, maintenance, and devices

Set up event spaces, move chairs and tables, clean halls and carpets, general maintenance, repair devices, assemble small appliances, test equipment, and organize parts.

Do you have your own business?

According to the business program described in the original guide, a company could receive compensation when its staff record approved tasks at work, in addition to continuing their usual operations.

How the program is described

The guide indicates a possible payment of $10 per employee per approved hour of recording. The owner would decide how to distribute that amount, subject to the official conditions of the business program.

Example from the guide

If 3 employees each record 5 approved hours in a week:

3 × 5 = 15 hours.

15 × $10 = $150 potential for that week, depending on the applicable conditions.

Which businesses could it work for?

Cleaning services, landscaping, restaurants, salons, and other companies that already do eligible work. Permission and compliance with all program rules are required.

How do payments work?

Sunday: hours deadline

The original guide notes that the hours you want to include in the week must be completed before the Sunday deadline. Check Amelia for the exact time and the applicable time zone.

Monday: request your payment

If you meet the $100 minimum in task earnings and the other requirements, you can request payment on Monday.

Monday to Friday: review

Submitted hours are reviewed for accuracy, compliance with requirements, and absence of fraudulent activity.

Friday: payment sent

The guide indicates that payments are sent on Fridays via PayPal or Tipalti, depending on availability and account setup.

Minimum withdrawal and referrals

  • Minimum task earnings to request payment: $100.
  • Minimum indicated to withdraw referral earnings: $50.
  • If you haven't reached the minimum yet, eligible earnings may remain in your account per the program terms.

Bonuses and challenges

Bonus and challenge conditions can change. Check the communications and the official app for current promotions.

Referrals: share and earn

You can share the opportunity with other people through your personal invitation link.

How they work

  • The guide indicates you could earn 10% of your direct referrals' earnings.
  • The referred person keeps their own earnings.
  • The regular accumulated commission may have a cap of $250 per person.

Example from the guide: If a referred person earns $100 per week and keeps that level for four weeks, their example total would be $400, and 10% would equal $40 in commissions for you, subject to the program rules.

“Captain” program

The original guide indicates that a person with more than 10 referrals who work 10 hours could request “Captain” status. This status may include commissions without the regular cap and additional incentives; confirm the current requirements and percentages before promoting them.
